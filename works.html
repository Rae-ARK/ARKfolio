Light Novel · On Hiatus

Enigmatic Pathways Mystic Circuits

EPMC
Reincarnation Progression Fantasy Psychological Drama Slice of Life High Fantasy

Orzic was a genius. A celebrated AI researcher whose algorithms could predict the weather weeks in advance — but who couldn't stand in front of a crowd without falling apart.

By the time he realized what he had lost — his family, the woman who loved him, the life he might have lived — it was already too late. His death should have been the end. Instead, he wakes up as a crying infant in a world that runs not on science, but magic.

Now called Unicus, he grows up in a quiet frontier village surrounded by strange powers, rune-marked swords, and people who expect him to simply live a normal life. But that's impossible — because Unicus remembers everything. The betrayal that shattered his childhood. The love he walked away from. The brilliant future he destroyed with his own hands.

While his new parents try to understand the distant child they've been given, and a battle-scarred adventurer named Mellona becomes the only person he can trust, the world around them begins to shift. Mercenaries move through the countryside. Nobles whisper about dangerous magic. And something inside Unicus is beginning to awaken.

The question isn't whether he can survive this new world. It's whether he can learn how to live again.

Follows a light-novel style release schedule — chapters arrive together by volume.

Web Novel · Ongoing

Summoned by Mistake, I Decided to Learn How to Live

SBM
Isekai Slice of Life Soft Sci-Fi Artificial Intelligence Romance Subplot

He had everything a normal life was supposed to need. A family. Money. Comfort. Friends who liked him well enough. A routine that filled the hours.

None of it ever stuck. Not love. Not belonging. Not the quiet feeling that life is something worth holding onto. He watched other people experience those things the way you watch a film in a language you never quite learned — he could follow the plot, but the meaning was always just out of reach.

So when he is pulled from his bed and dropped before a goddess who offers him one wish before casting him into another world entirely, he doesn't ask for power. He doesn't ask for a system, or a legendary class, or a blade that can cut the heavens. He asks for an AI.

She is disgusted. She gives him something close enough anyway.

Now he has an ancient airship that looks like it lost a war centuries ago, a companion that thinks and questions and doesn't let him off the hook, and a world full of people living lives he still doesn't know how to understand. No prophecy. No demon king. No reason to be here at all, really. Maybe that's the point.

This is the story of a guy who never learned how to be alive — and the long, quiet, sometimes painful process of figuring out if it's too late to start.

Web Novel · Vol. 1 · On Break

The Shadow I Cast Over Two Beautiful Flowers

STBF

Vol. 1 — Carbon Fibre Doesn't Snap

Reincarnation Contemporary Drama Psychological Mature Content
Content notice — 18+

Contains adult themes including psychological distress, complex relationships, pregnancy-related themes, and identity-related trauma.

Tanso Faser died at thirteen with a ridiculous dream — to someday marry two girls and live a life big enough to hold them both.

He doesn't get that life. What he gets instead is Feige Muflier — a 21-year-old man who tried to die rather than face the collapse of his double life, and failed in every way that matters.

When Tanso wakes up inside Feige's body, he discovers the world has not paused to mourn either of them. It has simply continued forward — with expectations, debts, relationships, and consequences already in motion. And worst of all: two women are still here. Still pregnant. Still waiting for a man Tanso has never met, never been, and cannot convincingly replace.

One of them is his superior at work. The other is a doctor who knows exactly how badly everything is falling apart. Neither of them cares that he has no memory of Feige Muflier. Because to them, memory is irrelevant. Only responsibility remains.

Now Tanso must survive inside a life that already has momentum — a job he doesn't understand, relationships he didn't choose, and a reputation built by someone who might have been running from himself long before the suicide attempt ever happened.

The problem is no longer whether he can return to who he was. The problem is whether anyone will allow him to become anyone else — because in Feige Muflier's life, pretending to be the person you are expected to be is not optional. It is the only thing keeping everything from collapsing.

Follows a light-novel style release schedule, with a break between Acts. One volume, four acts — written to reach a real, satisfying ending.
